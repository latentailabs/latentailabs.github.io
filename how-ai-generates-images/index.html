Every Clock Says 10:10? How AI Actually Generates Images — Diffusion, Latent Space & Flow Matching (Every Number Measured)

Diffusion, latent space and flow matching on FLUX.2 and SDXL, and why a 7:25 clock comes out as 10:10.

By Arun Prabhakaran · Originally published 22 Oct 2026 in Towards AI · 18 min full read

TL;DR

Image models start from random static and follow a learned velocity field back to a photo, fixing composition first and fine detail last, inside a compressed latent space that makes attention 65,536× cheaper. Text steers the process through a text encoder and classifier-free guidance. Clocks come out at 10:10 because the training photos are full of them, and fast 4-step distilled models snap to that most common mode.

Summary

Part 4 turns from text to pictures, running Black Forest Labs' open FLUX.2 models and Stable Diffusion XL on a 96 GB Blackwell GPU. It starts with three oddities: asked for a clock showing 7:25, the 4-step model put the main hands at 10:10 on all 8 seeds in an A100 re-run, sometimes adding a stray hand toward 7 or 5; a wine glass “filled to the brim” always stopped about half full; and an empty prompt still produced full scenes, from landscape collages to abstract art.

To a GPU, a 1024 × 1024 photo is 3,145,728 numbers, and almost every random point in that space is static. Training mixes an image with Gaussian noise along a straight line, and fine detail dies first: at σ = 0.30 only 4.3% of the fine-detail frequency energy still comes from the photo, while the low-frequency pose and horizon band is 99.99% signal. Generation therefore runs the other way, composition first and whiskers last; in the 50-step run the fox's pose is already fixed by Step 8.

The model works in a compressed latent space: 4,096 patch tokens instead of a million pixels, a 65,536× cut in attention cost. FLUX.2's 32-channel VAE keeps small text legible where SDXL's 4-channel one melts it (37.56 dB against 33.17 dB on a text chart). CLIP's 77-token limit silently dropped the last 19 tokens of a long prompt, while FLUX.2 reads prompts with Qwen3-4B. Classifier-free guidance pushes away from the empty-prompt prediction, with w = 4.0 as the sweet spot. Rectified-flow paths measured 95.12% straight, which is why a distilled 4-step model renders in 654 ms, 19.97× faster than 50 steps, and also why it snaps to the most common answer: 10:10.

Questions this article answers

Why do AI image generators always draw clocks at 10:10?

Most clock photos show 10:10, the time catalogues favour because it frames the logo, so 10:10 is a strong attractor for “clock”. Asked for 7:25, the 4-step distilled FLUX.2 model put the main hands at 10:10 on 8 of 8 seeds (several with a stray hand toward 7 or 5), and the 50-step base model with guidance 4.0 did so on 3 of 4 (A100 re-run). Few-step distillation likely strengthens that pull.

Why can't AI draw a wine glass filled to the brim?

The training prior wins. Almost every wine glass in photos is poured about half full, so the unconditional prediction pulls hard toward that level. Classifier-free guidance only extrapolates from the empty-prompt prediction toward your prompt, and “filled to the absolute brim” points into a region with almost no training examples. In the article's test, all 4 seeds stopped at roughly 50% full.

How does diffusion turn noise into an image?

Training blends a clean image with Gaussian noise along a straight line and teaches the network to predict the way back. Noise destroys fine detail first: at σ = 0.30, only 4.3% of the fox photo's fine-detail frequency energy was still signal, while the low-frequency structure was 99.99% signal. Generation runs this in reverse, fixing composition early, then shape, then texture. A 50-step FLUX.2 run produced the 1024 × 1024 image in 13.05 seconds on a Blackwell GPU.

What is latent space in Stable Diffusion and FLUX?

A compressed version of the image made by a variational autoencoder. A 1024 × 1024 picture is downsampled 8× per side and patchified into 4,096 latent tokens, so attention handles 16,777,216 pairs instead of about 1.10 trillion for raw pixels, a 65,536× reduction. How much the VAE keeps matters: SDXL's 4-channel latent melted 12pt text, while FLUX.2's 32-channel latent stayed sharp, scoring 37.56 dB against 33.17 dB.

What does the CFG scale do in AI image generation?

Classifier-free guidance runs the model twice per step, once with your prompt and once with an empty prompt, then pushes the result further in the direction that separates them. In the article's sweep (A100 re-run), w = 1.0 looked pale and washed out; saturation more than doubled by w = 4.0 (+116%), then barely rose by w = 15.0 (+126%).

Why does the seed control the layout of an AI image?

The seed fixes the starting grid of random noise, and rectified flow follows a deterministic path from it. Low-frequency bright and dark patches in that noise act like a map for the composition. Holding Seed 42 fixed and changing only “red fox” to “red cat” kept the same horizon, tree and sun, giving a 16.67 dB pixel match (0.835 correlation) with the fox image, against 11.46–13.52 dB for other seeds (A100 re-run).
